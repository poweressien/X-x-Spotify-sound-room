Today

  • A song on X is a link. People tap away and listen alone.
  • Spotify has no live room for a group. Listening together means a screen share or a call.

  • Voice, chat and reactions sit on top of the music, inside X.

        • Read your public X profile and followers
        • Send DMs you choose to send
        • Search Spotify and control your player

        What it cannot do

        • Read DMs you did not send through it
        • See your payment details on Spotify
        • Post on X without you pressing a button

        How it would be built

        A thin real-time service sits between listeners and two existing platforms. Audio never passes through it.

        Keeping everyone on the same beat

        1. The service holds the room clock: which track, which position, and when that was measured.
        2. Each device starts the track on its own Spotify player at that position, adjusted for how long the command took to arrive.
        3. Devices report drift. When one falls too far behind, the service sends a seek to catch it up.

        Spotify offers no shared clock, so sync is approximate. The target is within a second or two, to be tuned in a pilot.

        SoundRoom: feature concept

        Collaborative live listening for X and Spotify Premium

        Summary

        SoundRoom is a concept for live group listening on X. A member opens a room, links Spotify Premium, and listens with others: one queue, one chat, reactions, and an optional X Space for voice.

        The problem

        • Music sharing on X is passive. A link starts a solo listen.
        • Spotify has no live social layer for a group of strangers or fans.
        • X Spaces has voice, but no built-in music.

        What SoundRoom adds

        One room

        Voice, music, chat and reactions in one place, inside X.

        One queue

        Anyone adds songs. The room votes. The top song plays next.

        Open doors

        Public, request-to-join and private rooms. Artists can host listening parties.

        Why each side would want it

        For X

        A reason to open X during music moments, a Premium-worthy feature, and a place for music conversation to stay on X instead of leaving through a link.

        For Spotify

        A group-listening feature that needs Premium on every listener, with reach into X's audience and a new route to discovery for artists.

        Risks and dependencies

        • Spaces control. Creating or hosting a Space from another app is not in X's public API, so voice needs a first-party integration or a partnership.
        • Sync is approximate. Spotify has no shared clock. Expect a second or two of drift, corrected by periodic seeks.
        • Premium for all. Every listener needs Spotify Premium, and production use needs Spotify's approval and agreement to its developer terms.
        • Safety. Public rooms need host controls, reporting, rate limits and moderation from day one.

        Pilot plan

        1. 1

          Private beta

          Invite-only rooms with a few hosts and artists. Measure sync quality and session length.

        2. 2

          Request rooms

          Listening parties where the host approves guests. Measure return visits and Premium sign-ups.

        3. 3

          Public rooms

          Open rooms, genre browsing and the weekly leaderboard, with moderation tools live.

        Targets to test

        These are illustrative targets for a pilot to confirm or reject. They are not measurements.

        +18%Spotify Premium conversion
